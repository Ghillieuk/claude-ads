<p>"Midlayer" is one of those words that gets used constantly in outdoor clothing without anyone quite pausing to explain it. You've probably worked out roughly what it means from context — something you wear in the middle, presumably — but if you're stood in front of a rack trying to decide between a fleece, a quarter-zip and a gilet, "roughly" isn't much help. Here's the plain-English version, and a straight answer on which one actually suits you.</p>

<h2>The Three-Layer System, Without the Jargon</h2>
<p>Dressing for changeable British weather comes down to three jobs, done by three separate layers. The base layer sits against your skin and manages moisture — a T-shirt or a thin thermal, doing the least glamorous but most direct job. The outer layer is your shield against wind and rain — a coat or a proper shell, the thing that takes the weather so you don't have to. The midlayer sits between the two, and its job is simpler than either: trap warmth. That's it. It's not fighting the elements directly and it's not against your skin — it's just holding onto the heat your body's already making, so you're not relying on the outer layer to do a job it was never built for.</p>

<h2>So What Actually Counts as a Midlayer?</h2>
<p>In practice, a midlayer is usually one of three things: a fleece, a knitted quarter-zip or sweater, or a gilet. What they have in common is insulation without bulk — enough loft to hold warm air near your body, without being so thick that a coat won't fit over the top. <h2>Getting the Fit Right</h2>
<p>A midlayer needs room to do its job without being sized like an outer coat. Too snug and it can't trap a proper layer of warm air; too loose and it bunches under a coat instead of sitting flat. As a rule, it should sit comfortably over a T-shirt or thin base layer with a bit of ease through the shoulders and sleeves, so a coat can still go over the top without feeling tight across the arms. If you're between sizes on any of ours, sizing up is almost always the safer call for a midlayer — you can always push the sleeves up, but you can't add room that isn't there.</p>

<p>A midlayer's entire job is holding onto warmth you're already generating, which means the "best" one is whichever suits how you actually spend your day — a fleece for changeable outdoor stretches, a quarter-zip for everyday smart-casual layering, or a gilet when you want your arms free.
