## Body (paste-ready HTML)

<p>Most flannel advice online tells you what the fabric is good for — warmth, durability, that sort of thing. Fewer tell you what to actually put on with it. A flannel shirt on its own isn't an outfit, it's one piece of one. Here are three complete ways to wear yours this autumn, from a cold buttoned-up morning to a shirt doing outerwear duty on its own.</p>

<h2>Formula One: Buttoned Up, With Jeans and Boots</h2>
<p>The simplest version and the one most people default to — flannel buttoned to the collar or top button open, sleeves down, with dark jeans and a pair of proper boots. This is the formula for the coldest end of the day: a first walk before the frost has lifted, or standing around rather than moving. Our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/brae-flannel-shirt">Brae Flannel Shirt</a> is the one to reach for here — double-brushed 100% cotton flannel with enough weight to hold its own as the only layer on top, and a hidden button under the collar so it doesn't flap about in wind. Keep the rest of the outfit plain. The check is doing the visual work; it doesn't need competing with anything busy underneath.</p>

<h2>Formula Two: Open Over a Tee, Sleeves Rolled</h2>
<p>Once the day's actually warmed up, buttoned-to-the-collar stops making sense. Unbutton the flannel, wear it open over a plain T-shirt, and roll the cuffs back rather than leaving them loose — that's the difference between looking deliberate and looking like you forgot to do the shirt up. This is the formula for changeable mornings, the kind where you'll likely take a layer off before you're back. We've gone into the mechanics of this properly in <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/blogs/news/layering-a-t-shirt-under-a-flannel-or-overshirt">layering a T-shirt under a flannel or overshirt</a>, including how to get the collar and cuffs sitting right — worth a read if this is the formula you'll wear most.</p>

<h2>Formula Three: As the Outer Layer, Nothing Over It</h2>
<p>The lightest of the three shirts in our range works best as the whole outer layer rather than something to button all the way — worn open over a tee with nothing on top of it at all. The <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/munro-brushed-cotton-shirt">Munro Brushed Cotton Shirt</a> is cut closer through the chest and long through the body, which is exactly what you want from a shirt that's doing the job of a jacket rather than a base layer. This is the formula for a mild autumn afternoon or anywhere the temperature swings too much through the day to commit to a proper midlayer — on and off in seconds, and it packs down small enough to carry if you end up not needing it at all.</p>

<h2>Which Flannel for Which Formula</h2>
<p>The three formulas above aren't interchangeable with any shirt in the range — weight matters. The Brae Flannel is the heaviest of the three and the right call for Formula One, where it's carrying the whole outfit's warmth on its own. The Munro is the lightest and suits Formula Three, where it needs to move freely as an open outer layer rather than sit buttoned and static. Either works for Formula Two, depending on how cold the morning actually is. If you're still working out the difference between a flannel worn buttoned and one worn as an overshirt, our piece on <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/blogs/news/shacket-vs-overshirt-whats-the-difference">shacket vs overshirt</a> covers the terminology; our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/blogs/news/how-to-choose-a-flannel-shirt-the-mens-buying-guide">flannel shirt buying guide</a> covers picking one from scratch.</p>
