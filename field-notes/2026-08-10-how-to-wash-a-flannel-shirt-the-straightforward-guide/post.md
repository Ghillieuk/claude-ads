## Body (paste-ready HTML)

<p>Flannel has a reputation it doesn't quite deserve — somewhere between "dry clean only" and "handle with care" in most people's minds, when really it's one of the easier things in a layering system to look after. Ours goes straight in the washing machine. Here's how to do that properly, so the brushed nap stays soft and the check keeps its colour for years rather than a couple of washes.</p>

<h2>What's Actually in the Fabric</h2>
<p>Our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/brae-flannel-shirt">Brae Flannel Shirt</a> is a denser, double-brushed 100% cotton flannel — brushed on both sides for the softness and the bit of warmth flannel is known for. The <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/munro-brushed-cotton-shirt">Munro Brushed Cotton Shirt</a> is cut from the same family of fabric, a touch lighter. Being 100% cotton rather than a wool blend is the whole reason the care routine here looks nothing like our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/blogs/news/how-to-care-for-a-wool-gilet-a-no-fuss-guide">wool gilet care guide</a> — no dry cleaner required, no antibacterial fibres doing the work for you. It just needs a proper wash now and then, done the right way.</p>

<h2>The Short Version</h2>
<p>Wash at 30°C or on a cold cycle. Turn it inside out first. Skip the bleach and anything harsh. Hang it to dry rather than tumbling it. That's the routine — the rest of this is the reasoning, plus a couple of things most generic flannel-care advice online doesn't mention.</p>

<h2>Washing It Properly</h2>
<p>A 30°C wash or a cold cycle is what the care label calls for, and it's worth sticking to — hot water is what pulls the colour and softness out of a flannel shirt faster than anything else. Turn the shirt inside out before it goes in; the brushed nap and the dye sit on the outer face, and washing it inside out means the drum does its agitating against the plain side of the fabric instead. Wash with similar colours, keep the load loose rather than packed tight, and leave out the bleach and harsh detergents — plain, gentle detergent is all a 100% cotton flannel needs. One thing worth knowing before you buy rather than after: cotton garments shrink a small amount, typically 1-2%, in the first few washes, so it's factored into the sizing already rather than something to correct for.</p>

<h2>Drying and Ironing</h2>
<p>Hang it to dry rather than reaching for the tumble dryer — heat is the enemy of the brushed finish twice over, once in the wash and again in the drum, and a shirt dried flat or on a hanger holds its shape better besides. If it needs ironing, do it on the cotton setting and, again, inside out: pressing directly on the brushed face flattens the nap and leaves the check looking duller than it should.</p>

<h2>Between Washes</h2>
<p>Unlike wool, cotton flannel doesn't resist odour or dirt on its own, so don't expect to get away with airing it indefinitely the way you can with the gilet. A soft brush will still lift dry mud or dust before a wash is strictly necessary, and it's worth dealing with spills straight away — blot rather than rub, and let a proper wash handle the rest rather than scrubbing at it in the moment. Beyond that, just wash it when it needs it. There's no trick to extending the gap the way there is with wool.</p>

<h2>Storing It</h2>
<p>Hang it on a proper hanger rather than folding it into a drawer — flannel holds a crease more readily than it should if it's left balled up, and a hanger keeps the collar and shoulders in shape between wears. Give it a little room either side in the wardrobe rather than crushing it against heavier coats, and it'll come out ready to wear rather than needing a re-iron first.</p>

<h2>The Field Notes Verdict</h2>
<p>A flannel shirt is genuinely low-maintenance — 30°C, inside out, hang to dry, and you're most of the way there.
