## Body (paste-ready HTML)

<p>A beanie and a trucker cap don't really compete with each other — they solve different problems, and reaching for the wrong one is a small but avoidable way to end up either too hot under the brim or with cold ears by the second field. Here's the actual difference, and how we'd decide between our own.</p>

<h2>What Each One Is Actually Built For</h2>
<p>A beanie is a closed, ribbed knit with no ventilation built in — the whole point is to trap heat close to the head and cover the ears completely. Our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/highlander-beanie">Highlander Beanie</a> is exactly that: a wool-blend ribbed knit in charcoal grey, cut to sit snug rather than loose, built for the days when the wind's doing more damage than the temperature suggests.</p>
<p>A trucker cap is the opposite design brief. The mesh back is there to vent heat, not trap it, and the stiff front panel and peak are about shading your eyes and holding their shape, not warmth. Our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/original-trucker-cap-lichen-green">Original Trucker Cap</a> and the <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/camo-trucker-cap-bark">Camo Trucker Cap - Bark</a> are built to that brief — good in the cold as long as your ears aren't the problem, useless at keeping real cold off the top of your head.</p>

<h2>The Weather Call</h2>
<p>Once it's properly cold — frost on the ground, wind with some bite in it, the kind of morning where your ears start hurting before the rest of you notices — the beanie wins outright. There's no version of a trucker cap that fixes cold ears; the mesh that makes it useful in October is the exact thing working against you in January. Above that threshold, on a bright, mild or just overcast day where the issue is sun and drizzle rather than genuine cold, the cap is the better call. It breathes, it doesn't leave you overheating on a brisk walk, and it does a proper job of keeping low sun out of your eyes in a way a beanie can't.</p>

<h2>The Activity Call</h2>
<p>Pace matters as much as temperature. A fast dog walk or anything that gets your heart rate up generates its own heat fast, and a beanie can go from useful to stifling in the first ten minutes — that's when the cap's ventilation earns its keep even on a coolish day. Standing still is the opposite case: waiting at a gate, watching a field, anything where you're not generating heat yourself, and the beanie's full coverage stops the cold finding you the way it will under a mesh back. If you're layering a hood over the top of either, the beanie sits flatter and doesn't fight the hood's shape the way a structured cap's front panel can.</p>

<h2>The Field Notes Verdict</h2>
<p>Don't treat this as a style choice. Cold and stationary, the beanie wins; mild, active or bright, the cap does. Keep both in the car or by the door and let the actual morning decide, rather than whichever one you grabbed on the way out.</p>
