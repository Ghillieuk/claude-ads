## Body (paste-ready HTML)

<p>The shoulder season is the trickiest stretch of the year to dress for. Cold enough at seven in the morning to want something proper on, warm enough by eleven that the same something is a mistake. A flannel shirt on its own is usually the wrong answer either way — too thin at dawn, too much by midday. The fix isn't a different shirt, it's what goes underneath it.</p>

<h2>Why a T-Shirt, Not a Base Layer</h2>
<p>Technical base layers are built for a very specific problem — moving hard in genuinely cold conditions — and most shoulder-season days in Britain aren't that. A plain cotton T-shirt does the job better here: light enough to sit under a flannel without adding bulk, and easy to peel the flannel off entirely once the day warms up without looking like you've just taken off a thermal in a pub car park. Our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/collections/t-shirts">Original Long Sleeve Tee</a> is built for exactly this — soft cotton with enough structure to sit flat under a shirt, not so thin it goes see-through the first time it gets a proper wash.</p>

<h2>Getting the Collar and Cuffs Right</h2>
<p>The difference between this looking deliberate and looking like you forgot a layer comes down to two details. First, the collar — worn open, a T-shirt collar should sit just inside the flannel's, not poking above it. Second, the cuffs — roll the flannel's sleeves back over the T-shirt's, not the other way round, so you get one clean line at the wrist instead of two competing hems. Neither takes any real effort, but skipping them is the fastest way to look like you're wearing pyjamas under a shirt rather than layering with intent.</p>

<h2>Reading the Actual Morning</h2>
<p>Treat it as a two-decision system rather than one outfit. First, is it a buttoned-up day or an open one — that's temperature, not preference, and it can change between the walk to the car and getting back. Second, which shirt weight matches the coldest part of the day you'll actually be out in, not the average. It's easier to leave a shirt open or push cuffs up if you've overdressed slightly than to manufacture warmth you didn't bring with you.</p>
