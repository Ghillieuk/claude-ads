## Body (paste-ready HTML)

<p>A wool beanie is one of the simpler things in a layering system to look after, right up until someone puts it through a hot wash or leaves it drying on a radiator — and then it's a beanie that no longer fits the way it did. None of the actual care is difficult. Most of it is just doing less than you'd instinctively do to a cotton T-shirt, and stopping there.</p>

<h2>What's Actually in the Knit</h2>
<p>Our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/highlander-beanie">Highlander Beanie</a> is a wool-blend ribbed knit, built to sit snug rather than loose and to hold that shape through repeated wear. Wool is the fibre doing most of the work here — it's what makes the beanie warm without being bulky and what lets it go longer between washes than you'd expect. The blend also means it behaves a little differently from a pure wool garment: it's more forgiving of a careful hand wash than something like our Tech Wool gilet, which is dry clean only, but it still doesn't want anywhere near a hot cycle or a tumble dryer.</p>

<h2>The Short Version</h2>
<p>Wash it far less often than you think it needs. When it does need washing, do it by hand in cool water with a wool-friendly detergent. Never wring it, never tumble dry it, and dry it flat rather than on a radiator or a hook. That's the whole routine — the rest of this is the reasoning behind it.</p>

<h2>Why It Needs Washing Less Than You'd Think</h2>
<p>Wool is naturally antibacterial and odour-resistant, the same property that makes our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/blogs/news/how-to-care-for-a-wool-gilet-a-no-fuss-guide">wool gilet</a> such low-maintenance kit. A beanie that's had a cold walk on it doesn't need washing afterwards — it needs airing. Leave it somewhere ventilated, out of direct heat, for a few hours before it goes back in a drawer, and that alone accounts for most of what a beanie needs between proper washes. Reach for an actual wash only when there's a visible mark or it's genuinely started to smell, not on a fixed schedule.</p>

<h2>How to Actually Wash It</h2>
<p>Fill a sink or basin with cool water and a small amount of mild, wool-specific detergent — nothing formulated for cotton or synthetics, which is often harsher than wool wants. Submerge the beanie and move it gently through the water for a minute or two; don't scrub it and don't rub the knit against itself, which is how ribbing loses its shape. Leave it to soak for up to half an hour if it needs more than a quick clean, then rinse in water the same temperature as the wash — a sudden change in temperature between washing and rinsing is one of the more common ways wool ends up felted and shrunken.</p>

<h2>Drying It Without Ruining the Shape</h2>
<p>Never wring a wet beanie — twisting the knit is a fast way to distort it permanently. Instead, press it between two clean towels to lift out the excess water, then reshape it by hand and lay it flat on a dry towel somewhere out of direct sun and away from radiators or other direct heat. Wool loses its shape under heat far more readily than it does under cold water, so patience here matters more than the wash itself. Leave it flat until it's fully dry, which is usually the better part of a day — resist the urge to speed it up.</p>

<h2>What Not to Do</h2>
<p>Skip the washing machine unless you're prepared to accept some risk to the fit — a wool blend can survive a gentle, cold, wool-cycle wash in a laundry bag, but hand washing removes that risk entirely and takes barely longer. Don't tumble dry it under any circumstances, don't hang it to dry on a hook or peg (the weight of the wet knit will stretch it out of shape), and don't leave it damp and balled up in a coat pocket after a wet walk — get it out and laid flat as soon as you're in.</p>

<h2>Storing It Between Wears</h2>
<p>Fold it flat rather than hanging it — a beanie left on a hook for weeks will slowly stretch under its own weight, and a ribbed knit is slower to spring back than most people expect. A drawer or a shelf, folded once or laid flat, keeps the shape true for next time. If it's going into off-season storage for a few months, make sure it's fully clean and dry first; anything left on a knit over a long stretch tends to set in rather than brush out.</p>

<h2>The Field Notes Verdict</h2>
<p>A wool beanie asks for less than most of the things in your wardrobe, not more — wash it rarely, wash it cold and by hand when you do, and let it dry flat rather than rushing it.
