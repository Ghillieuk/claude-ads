## Body (paste-ready HTML)

<p>A gilet keeps your core warm but leaves your arms exposed. A down jacket solves that, but it's built for the field, not for wherever you end up after it — the pub, a farm shop, somewhere you'd rather not turn up looking like you're still out walking the dog. The <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/arkle-wool-jacket">Arkle Wool Jacket</a> sits in the gap between those two: a proper jacket with sleeves, smart enough that you don't need to change out of it, still practical enough for the walk there. Here's what it's actually made of, and when it's the right call over the other two.</p>

<h2>What Is the Arkle Wool Jacket?</h2>
<p>Ghillie describes it as sitting "somewhere between a jacket and an overshirt," and the construction backs that up. The main fabric is a Tech Wool blend — 60% wool, 32% polyester, 3% acrylic, 3% polyamide and 2% viscose — the same proprietary fabric used in the <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/collections/midlayers">Torr Wool Gilet</a>, giving it a similar structured hand-feel but in a full-sleeved cut with a 100% cotton lining. The detailing is where it earns the "not saved for best" line Ghillie uses to describe it: horn-style branded buttons with button cuffs, two pleated chest patch pockets, two deep waist pockets closed with YKK Vislon zips and custom logo pulls, plus two open-top pockets inside for the things you don't want zipped away. A contrast brushed cotton inner neck and zip guard, and a matched-thread logo embroidered on the left shoulder, round it out. It runs S to 3XL, care is a straightforward 30°C or cold wash with no tumble drying, no bleach and no harsh chemicals — allow for the usual 1-2% shrinkage you'd expect from any cotton-lined garment.</p>

<h2>Wool Jacket or Wool Gilet?</h2>
<p>The honest answer is sleeves. The <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/collections/midlayers">Torr Wool Gilet</a> is built to layer under something else — a flannel shirt, an overshirt, a coat — and it does that job well precisely because it's sleeveless and low-bulk. The Arkle isn't a layering piece in the same sense; it's the outer layer itself. If you're stacking warmth under a coat, the gilet is still the right tool. If you want one thing to put on and be done — smart enough to not look like you're mid-layering-system, warm enough on its own for a crisp but not brutal day — that's the Arkle's job, not the gilet's.</p>

<h2>Wool Jacket or Down Jacket?</h2>
<p>Where the <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/corrie-down-jacket">Corrie Down Jacket</a> is built for genuinely cold, exposed conditions — goose down fill, water-repellent shell, the piece you reach for on the coldest days of the layering system — the Arkle is built for versatility rather than maximum warmth. It's less bulky, has none of the puffer silhouette, and the wool-blend construction with a cotton lining reads as a proper jacket rather than technical outerwear. On a genuinely foul day, the down jacket wins. On a normal cold-but-manageable day where you might end up somewhere other than a muddy field, the Arkle does more of the work — you're not standing in a pub in a jacket built for a blizzard.</p>

<h2>Styling It From the Field to the Pub</h2>
<p>The jacket's whole case rests on not needing to change halfway through the day, so build the rest of the outfit around that logic. Underneath, a <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/collections/flannels">flannel or check shirt</a> gives it something to layer over without adding bulk under the sleeves — worn open, the Arkle reads closer to a smart overshirt than a technical jacket. Jeans or cords and a pair of boots that can survive a farm track carry it through both ends of the day. Leave it buttoned and it looks deliberate rather than thrown on; wear it open over a shirt and it drops a register, which is really the point of a hybrid piece like this — it's meant to move between those two states without you having to think about it.</p>
