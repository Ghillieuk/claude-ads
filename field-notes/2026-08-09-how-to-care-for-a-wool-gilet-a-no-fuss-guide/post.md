## Body (paste-ready HTML)

<p>"Dry clean only" has a way of sounding like a threat. Say it about a garment and most people quietly file it under "too much effort" and reach for something machine-washable instead. Which is a shame, because a wool gilet is one of the lowest-maintenance things you can put in a layering system — it just needs looking after differently, not more often. Here's what that actually means.</p>

<h2>What's Actually in the Fabric</h2>
<p>Our <a href="https://ghillieuk.com/products/torr-wool-gilet-peat">Torr Wool Gilet</a> is built from a Tech Wool blend we spent two years developing — wool for the warmth and the way it regulates temperature, blended with a small amount of polyester, acrylic, polyamide and viscose to help it hold its shape and shrug off the wear a pure wool piece can struggle with. That blend is why the gilet is dry clean only rather than hand-washable: the wool content behaves differently in water than the synthetic fibres it's mixed with, and dry cleaning is the method that keeps both in good order without one shrinking or distorting against the other.</p>

<h2>The Short Version</h2>
<p>Dry clean it when it genuinely needs cleaning, not on a schedule. Air it between wears instead of washing it. Keep fastenings done up when it goes to the cleaners, so the shape holds. That's most of what there is to know — the rest of this is really just the reasoning behind those three habits.</p>

<h2>Why Wool Needs Cleaning Less Than You'd Think</h2>
<p>Wool is naturally antibacterial, which is the detail that makes the "dry clean only" label far less of a burden than it first sounds. It resists picking up the odours that send a lot of other fabrics straight to the wash after one wear, so a gilet that's had a day in the field doesn't necessarily need cleaning at all — it usually just needs airing. Hang it up somewhere with a bit of ventilation, out of direct sun, and give it a few hours before it goes back in the wardrobe. For most wear, that's the entire care routine.</p>

<h2>Between Wears — the Bit Most Care Guides Skip</h2>
<p>A soft clothes brush, run over the gilet lengthwise, will lift dried mud and surface dust before either has a chance to work into the weave. Do this after a walk rather than before the next one and you'll go a lot longer between actual dry cleans. If something's spilled on it, don't rub — blot it with a clean, dry cloth and deal with the rest at the cleaners rather than trying to treat a stain yourself. Wool can react badly to spot-cleaning products that would be fine on cotton.</p>

<h2>What Not to Do</h2>
<p>Skip the washing machine entirely, even on a wool or delicate cycle — the Tech Wool blend is specified as dry clean only for a reason, and a machine cycle risks shrinking or distorting it in a way that doesn't reverse. Don't tumble dry it, don't iron it directly, and don't leave it balled up in a bag after a wet walk — if it does get properly soaked, hang it out flat to dry naturally before it goes anywhere near a wardrobe.</p>

<h2>Storing It Between Seasons</h2>
<p>A gilet earns its keep by being worn most of the year, but if it does get a spell in the back of the wardrobe, hang it on a proper hanger rather than folding it over a rail — that keeps the shoulders from creasing and the shape true. Give it room either side rather than crushing it between heavier coats, and it'll come out ready to wear rather than needing a steam and a brush-down first.</p>

<h2>The Field Notes Verdict</h2>
<p>"Dry clean only" isn't a mark against a wool gilet — it's just a different, and honestly lighter, kind of upkeep than you're used to with everyday clothing. Air it, brush it, dry clean it occasionally, and a well-made wool gilet like ours will outlast most things in the wardrobe that need washing every week.
